# Fleet resilience: salvage, insurance, and rechargeable shields

## Purpose

Incursion losses now create three different economic responses instead of one automatic bailout:

1. The titled wreck produces a public salvage contract.
2. A fleet policy may pay part of the destroyed hull's capital loss.
3. The operator still funds the deductible and uncovered replacement cost, using cash or fleet finance.

These are intentionally separate transactions. A hull claim does not confer salvage authority, and towing a wreck does not replace the ship.

## Institutional salvage

First Reach Recovery reads the same offered `wreck-salvage` contracts shown on the public board. When Blue Hook is available and SPRC can fund the posted recovery reward without crossing its reserve, Nell accepts one job, receives authority limited to that titled wreck, recovers it to Scrap Porch, and is paid by SPRC. Delivery retires the wreck title and adds two iron-nickel plus one silicate to SPRC raw inventory.

The initial travel is time-and-distance based rather than fully rendered physical towing. The contract, authority, payment, title retirement, inventory yield, provider availability, and wreck removal are real. A later presentation slice can put Blue Hook and the wreck on the physical route without changing the institutional lifecycle.

## Fleet insurance

First Reach Mutual is a stateful `fleet-insurer` institution with an operating reserve. Eligible hauling and mining businesses receive fleet policies. The initial policy has these tuning values:

- Premium: 6% of qualifying operating revenue.
- Hull coverage: 60% of the reference 6,000-credit hull cost.
- Deductible: 600 credits.
- Maximum claim: 3,600 credits.
- Underwriter protected reserve: 5,000 credits.
- Loss adjustment: each paid loss increases the premium rate by 1.5 percentage points, capped at 14%.

Claims are bounded by the insurer's spendable reserve. They pay the owning institution, after which existing replacement-funding logic determines whether remaining cash and borrowing can commission another craft. This deliberately prevents insurance from becoming infinite free replacement money.

## SPRC rechargeable shield service

An insured loss marks the affected fleet as high risk. When one of that fleet's living craft is physically at Scrap Porch, it may buy Sal's shield installation if:

- the owner can pay 1,400 credits without crossing its protected operating reserve;
- SPRC has one machine part and one silicate;
- the craft does not already have a shield.

The installed shield carries 72 charge. Incoming damage consumes charge point-for-point before reaching hull. It is damage capacity, not invulnerability. A depleted shield does nothing until recharged.

At a completed commercial stop, a shielded craft buys missing charge for 2 credits per unit, limited by what its owner can afford. This makes safety a capital and operating decision: installation reduces expected hull losses but raises the cost of every protected route.

## Important next observations

- Does First Reach Mutual remain solvent under sustained director pressure?
- Do reduced replacement loans outweigh premiums and recharge costs for cautious operators?
- Does Nell clear wrecks faster than attacks create them?
- Does SPRC's shield work compete with ordinary repairs for machine parts and silicate without starving the repair berth?
- Should operators decline, raise deductibles, or let policies lapse after repeated losses?
- When a second recovery provider exists, salvage selection should compare price, distance, commitments, and relationship rather than using one available provider.
